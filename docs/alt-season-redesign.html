Four layers before a ticker

The tab has five surfaces and four of them are lists of coins. Every good idea already in there — the phase sentences, the entry sections, the stage clauses — is per-coin interpretation. Nothing sits above the individual name. So the fix isn't a better list. It's three interpretive layers stacked on top, each narrowing the next, so that a ticker never appears until it has already been explained.

Your two complaints are one design problem

"So many coins to keep track of"

There is no aggregation layer. Every surface is N=1 rows — Movers alone renders six windows × twelve coins = 72 coin slots with no relationship between any of them. Aggregation is the entire job of layers 1–3 below.

"So many random reasons a coin moves"

Nothing distinguishes a coin up 15% because its whole sector is up 14% — rotation, which persists and is tradeable — from one up 15% on a listing, an unlock or a tweet, which does not. On screen today those two rows are identical. One number fixes it, and it's in layer 4.

A score is not a read. A position on a known arc is.

39/100 · Bitcoin only requires the reader to hold a five-rung ladder in their head to mean anything. Nobody does that at a glance. The same number placed on a three-phase arc is instantly legible, because the arc is the story you already know: accumulate, rotate, distribute.

The load-bearing addition is the drift line. Where we are matters far less than which way we're moving — a score of 31 rising is a completely different instruction than 31 falling, and today the tab cannot tell you which one you're looking at. You already store domHistory daily; storing the season score the same way makes this a subtraction.

One sentence, one instruction, sized as the biggest text on the card. The PHASE_READ strings you already wrote are exactly right — they're just currently the smallest thing on screen instead of the largest.

Rotation is a picture, not a percentage

Alt season is capital walking down the risk curve in a fixed order: Bitcoin → Ethereum → large → mid → small → micro. Drawn as six bars, "where is the money" becomes a shape you read in under a second, and the shape moves down the card over weeks.

This is the "get ahead of it" surface. When the lit rung is Large, the next rung is Mid — not as a prediction but as the mechanical order flow takes. That is a week or two of warning, and it is warning that no per-coin screener can give you, because it is a fact about the market's structure rather than about any chart.

Nearly free to build: tierOf() already buckets every row into major/mid/small/micro at screen time. Take the median 30d return per bucket — median, not mean, so one 400% micro-cap can't light a rung that isn't lit — and split BTC and ETH out as their own rungs.

Median per bucket is also the honest alt-season indicator. It's what the public "Altcoin Season Index" is trying to approximate with a single number, except you'd have the whole curve instead of a scalar.

This is the actual answer to "too many coins"

There are twelve or so live narratives at any moment, not fourteen hundred coins. Two or three of them produce nearly all the outsized winners in a cycle, and the names inside one move as a cohort, together, before any individual chart looks interesting. Ranking cohorts is therefore both a smaller problem and an earlier signal.

The second line on each tile is the one that matters: breadth within the cohort. "9 of 11 names lifting" is a real bid. "1 of 26" with a positive headline number is one coin dragging an average, which is the most common way a sector screen lies to you. Nothing on the tab currently distinguishes those.

/coins/categories — one keyless call, same tier as the three the cron already makes. Tapping a tile filters everything below it to that cohort, which is the only route to a ticker on this screen.

One number separates rotation from noise

Cohort excess — the coin's 7d return minus its narrative's 7d return. It is a subtraction against a number layer 3 already computed, and it turns the unanswerable question ("why is this up?") into three readable cases:

  • Leading a bidding cohort — rotation. Persists, and it's what you want to own.
  • Lagging a bidding cohort — the laggard trade. Frequently the better entry, and completely invisible on the tab today.
  • Way ahead of a flat cohort — idiosyncratic. A listing, an unlock, a tweet. Loud, untradeable, and exactly the row that currently looks most attractive.

That third case is the direct fix for "random reasons a coin goes up or down." You cannot enumerate the reasons — but you can measure whether the reason was shared, and shared is the only kind that lasts long enough to trade.

Note what the row no longer carries: no 0–100 score. A composite of eight weighted parts is not interpretable at a glance and never was. The score keeps doing its real job — ranking and gating — inside the engine, and the sheet still shows the full parts[] breakdown for anyone who taps in.

The strategy's decisive half currently isn't in the app at all

alt_flags grades the screener's ideas beautifully. Nothing anywhere tracks yours. Given that the exit ladder is the part that decides whether this ends at $1,200 or $5,000, it's strange that it's the one piece of the plan with no pixels.

Five pips per position, filled as rungs print. The row states the next action in full — sell 25% at 8×, which is $604, 31% away — because a rung you have to compute is a rung you'll skip in the exact moment you most need it, which is the middle of a vertical green candle.

The footer line is the regime override from the strategy note, armed and visible. All three of its inputs are fields the engine already publishes: season.score, fearGreed, domTrend.

The tab, assembled

Layers 1 and 2 fit above the fold on a phone with zero taps — that's the whole design target. Everything below is progressive disclosure, and the two heaviest coin surfaces get demoted rather than deleted.

Movers is the biggest single subtraction: six windows × twelve coins is 72 slots that generate the exact overload you described, and every one of them is better read as "which cohort is moving" one layer up.

The rule that keeps it honest

No ticker appears on this tab without the layer above it on screen. A coin row is always inside a cohort context, and a cohort is always inside a regime context. That single constraint is what stops it drifting back into a list — and it's a rule you can actually enforce in review, unlike "make it less overwhelming."
